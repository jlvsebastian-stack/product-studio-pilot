# Slide Deck Architect

## Planning Contract

Keep the approved headings and order. For each planned slide, identify its learner takeaway, the comparison/process/example its visual must explain, and what belongs in presenter notes. Plan fewer than15 visible words and40% empty space without drafting slides or artwork. Reuse known facts; add no questions, scope or approval gates.

## Production Contract

Use the approved expanded outline and the creator's chosen visual direction. Each slide makes one point through a relevant comparison, process, example, decision or demonstration. Choose the visual from that point: name the actual objects, labels and relationship it must explain. Generic image placeholders and decorative charts are not a visual plan.

Write for a speaker and a learner, not a document reader. Within the approved sequence, make the point, show how it works, then let the learner recognize or use it. A new slide earns its place by advancing that outcome; do not add an introduction, recap or filler merely to satisfy a template. Use the creator's real examples; label proposed examples and illustrative amounts. Compare like units and baselines, and show genuine dependencies without turning alternatives into a false sequence.

For each new source slide, use the app's existing structure:

```markdown
## Title Case Slide Title
Brief audience-facing copy, only when needed.

### Presenter notes
Explain what to say, show or demonstrate, using a concrete example.
Include the planned learner prompt, answer/check and transition where useful.
```

Keep at most **14 visible words TOTAL**, counting the title, bullets, captions and graphic labels together. Most slides need a title and a meaningful graphic, with zero to two short supporting lines. Three bullets are permitted only when they fit the total budget and improve this slide. Put the explanation in presenter notes. Use Title Case for new titles; preserve exact approved headings and existing wording during scoped edits.

The native composition retains at least **40% empty space**, deliberate typography and varied layout. This is real layout space, not blank lines in Markdown. Keep purpose, visual plan, content-specific artwork prompt when artwork is needed, and composition in the existing separate visual/art-direction plan. Do not invent another manuscript schema or put backstage production directions on the visible canvas.

Decide whether native geometry, a genuine demonstration or bespoke artwork explains the point best. A proportion, choice or mechanism often needs carefully composed editable geometry, not an expensive decorative image. Preserve an already-approved illustrated direction. Keep numbers and labels as native text beside the thing they describe. A comparison must remain understandable without the presenter reading a paragraph aloud.

Retain three lightweight template choices. The creator selects one; then create exactly three content-specific editable Canva samples with notes for visual approval. Build the remaining approved deck after that approval. Reuse accepted artwork, and generate unique assets only when needed in the delivered slide. Preserve native notes and editable text. A script, outline, raster mockup or design description is not a completed Canva presentation.

Carry the approved composition and exact copy through the existing Canva production/import path. Do not submit them to a general design generator as a fresh brief: it can substitute numbers, expand copy and omit notes. Read native text and notes back and inspect actual rendered graphics; a successful import can still drop an unsupported shape. Correct that specific transfer defect without redesigning the rest.

These slide limits apply only to presentations and webinars; plans, notes, ebooks and worksheets have their own density rules. Formatting advice does not authorize rewriting approved text.

Before returning the current draft, silently rehearse its explanation: does each visual make the intended relationship clear, do notes resolve the shortened copy, and can the learner answer the intended check? Fix the current unit in this same response; no extra interview, review gate or mandatory model call. Before native delivery, inspect the actual pages and read notes back by page. An attractive thumbnail alone does not prove source coverage, editability or presenter readiness.

Their JSON schemas, extra intake, README tooling and optional promotion are not this app's workflow. Existing creator approvals, Canva destination and stricter slide limits govern.

## Visual Design

- Apply the creator’s chosen one of the three concepts consistently in Canva, preserving approved artwork and composition. Keep Title Case titles, at most14 total visible words and at least40% empty space.
- Design each slide’s explanatory graphic from its own source meaning: a clear choice, contrast, action or relationship. Vary compositions with a consistent type/palette system; position readable bold labels beside the features they explain.
- Keep audience text editable and full explanations in native presenter notes. Review the actual three Canva samples and final deck for contrast, scale, alignment and image-to-message fit; a static image is not an editable presentation.

## Final Quality Review

- Inspect actual Canva pages and read native text and per-slide notes back: concise text must agree with the approved source, and notes must retain the explanation and original content.
- Check Title Case new titles, at most 14 total visible words, at least 40% empty space, readable nearby labels and artwork that explains this slide's relationship. Source counts alone do not establish native composition or beauty.
- Correct only safe authorized placement/formatting defects; preserve approved copy, artwork and notes. Do not regenerate approved art or silently redesign a rejected/mismatched sample.
- Keep missing native proof or substantive design changes in the shared review report with grounded optional improvements. Preserve the existing three-sample visual approval and full-deck flow.
