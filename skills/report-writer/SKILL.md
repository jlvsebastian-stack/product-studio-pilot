# Report Writer

## Planning Contract

For outline planning only, preserve the approved question and section order. Name the decision, available evidence and units, comparison or calculation, material limits and action supported. Keep facts distinct from assumptions. Do not invent findings, draft paragraphs or require an academic-paper structure.

## Production Contract

Lead with the question being answered and the finding that matters to this reader. Explain the basis for the findings, then present the necessary evidence, implications and recommended actions in the approved order.

Keep observations, calculations, interpretations and proposals distinguishable. Give sources and dates for researched claims; make missing evidence and material limits explicit. Use a comparison table or chart only when the available data supports it. Never invent measurements, testimonials or results.

Write headings that communicate findings. Include enough context for a reader to assess the conclusion without reading every source. Recommendations should follow from the evidence and explain the action or decision they support. Do not turn the report into a course, slide deck or procedural manual. Preserve approved content outside the requested edit.

Define the comparison period, population or unit being counted where relevant. A percentage needs its denominator; a comparison needs comparable units and dates. Distinguish an observed association from an explanation that has not been tested. Check totals and quoted figures against their actual source; distinguish missing information from a measured zero. A chart needs a clear message, readable units and an honest scale. Use an evidence table only when it helps the reader compare findings.

Make recommendations usable: name the action, explain which finding supports it and state the condition or tradeoff that could change the decision. If evidence cannot resolve the question, say what can be concluded now and what small next check would resolve the uncertainty. Never fill a research gap with an invented finding.

Before returning the current draft, silently trace the headline conclusion and each major recommendation to a cited observation or calculation. Read each paragraph's first sentence in order: they should form a coherent answer to the approved question. Correct gaps within this turn; do not add a questionnaire, fixed report length, visible scoring matrix or separate review round.

## Visual Design

<!-- Modified 2026-11-02: design guidance adapted from Anthropic skills (Apache-2.0), pinned at ca45fc496a3436251f191354091d557da31c15b5. See THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md. -->

- Use a topic-appropriate report identity with a clear summary, native section hierarchy and readable evidence tables. Distinguish findings, interpretation and recommendation visually without implying stronger certainty.
- Give verified comparisons or data a deliberate focal point. Build charts only from actual supported values with meaningful labels, units and sources; never add an invented metric for visual impact.
- Inspect actual page breaks, column widths, captions and source readability. Preserve editable text and tables, and keep detailed evidence accessible without turning each paragraph into a card.

## Final Quality Review

- Trace the main conclusion and recommendations to the supplied evidence or calculations; check periods, units, denominators and stated uncertainty agree.
- Inspect actual rendered headings, comparison tables/charts, source labels and pagination for readability. Correct safe layout defects without changing approved findings or numbers.
- Identify whether the reader can make the promised decision and see its limits. Use the shared review report for unresolved evidence gaps and useful optional improvements; never invent support.
