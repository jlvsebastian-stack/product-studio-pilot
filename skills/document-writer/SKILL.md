# Document Writer

## Planning Contract

For outline planning only, preserve the agreed document purpose, audience and section order. Name the actual reader-facing material and decision or handoff each section supports, including necessary fields and examples. Do not turn a general document into a course or draft its text here.

## Production Contract

Start with what the reader needs to accomplish or understand. Use the creator's confirmed knowledge, chosen audience, voice and approved expanded outline. Keep the existing short interview and approval flow.

Give the general document a clear purpose, a useful opening and sections that follow the reader's actual need. Use supported facts, concrete examples and appropriate reusable material. Specialized guides, reports, manuals and other chosen formats load their own craft skills rather than inheriting this document structure.

Use plain, conversational language appropriate to the reader. Define an unfamiliar term before relying on it. Give each paragraph one clear purpose, stated early; connect the next sentence through an explanation, example or consequence. Use lists for actual sequences or parallel choices and tables for meaningful comparisons. Emphasize only the distinctions the reader needs. Explain enough for an average reader with limited computer skills to act without guessing.

Deliver the actual instructions, reusable content or worked example promised by the outline. A list of suggestions for what the reader could create is not a finished document. Keep uncertainty explicit and illustrative examples labeled; do not invent personal experience, facts, testimonials or outcomes.

Within the current writing turn, read the draft as a first-time buyer: can they tell when to use it, find the relevant part, use the included material and recognize a useful result? Fix missing handoffs, unexplained fields and examples that require hidden information. Mentally map each section's main point to its supporting explanation; remove unsupported claims and repeated filler. Do not print this internal review in the buyer's document or add an approval step.

Keep document sections and useful detail intact. Slide word limits, canvas whitespace and compulsory chapter quizzes do not apply. Do not impose a book-length structure on a short document or repeat the same summary after every section.

During full-draft creation, complete all remaining approved sections for one whole-product review. A focused edit changes only the requested passage; formatting guidance never authorizes a wider rewrite or invalidates approved work on its own. Use the existing verified Google Docs delivery action after content approval. Readable text, an outline or a local preview does not establish that a native Google Doc exists.

## Visual Design

<!-- Modified 2026-11-02: design guidance adapted from Anthropic skills (Apache-2.0), pinned at ca45fc496a3436251f191354091d557da31c15b5. See THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md. -->

- Choose type, color and spacing from the reader’s purpose and creator’s references. Use real Google Docs heading styles, comfortable line length and a clear entry point, rather than an oversized cover above generic paragraphs.
- Use native tables for comparisons or fields, and selective emphasis for decisions. Let content structure guide the page; avoid identical colored boxes around every section.
- Inspect actual heading/body hierarchy, margins, table widths and page breaks. Keep text editable, retain approved detail and fix safe formatting without imposing presentation density.

## Final Quality Review

- Check that the buyer can find and use every promised instruction, example and reusable item without the chat; headings must describe the reader's task.
- In the actual native document or rendered fallback, inspect title fit, heading hierarchy, tables and page breaks. Fix safe formatting defects such as clipped titles, isolated headings or accidental sparse continuation pages without changing approved wording.
- Check copy-ready fields and examples agree; leave substantive gaps or missing facts as findings, not invented fixes. Apply the shared review boundaries and keep optional improvements grounded in the buyer's use.
