# Checklist Craft

## Planning Contract

For outline planning only, preserve the agreed task and sequence. Name the moment of use, observable checks, pass/fail meaning and corrective action or stop condition. Plan a reusable checklist rather than tips or a lecture; add no intake, scoring system or approval.

## Production Contract

State when the checklist is used and what readiness or completion it establishes. Group checks by the actual workflow, timing or decision. Each item names one observable condition with a clear pass criterion; vague reminders such as be prepared are insufficient.

Use checkboxes or a compact check/result/action table. Include a brief correction or escalation where failure changes what the reader should do. Distinguish required checks from optional improvements and use not-applicable choices only when meaningful.

Include a short filled or difficult-case example where it helps the buyer interpret the checks. Keep explanations close to the relevant item. Deliver the actual checklist rather than an essay about making one. Preserve the creator's method, approved scope and edit boundaries.

Phrase checks as verifiable conditions, not aspirations. For example, replace "Confirm details" with the actual details the reader must match and where to find them. Keep one decision per checkbox; split combined checks when one part could pass while another fails. Use a blank to record an answer only when that answer affects the next action. Include an owner or completion date only when the workflow needs accountability.

Order prerequisites before dependent checks. Identify checks that block proceeding, the correction available to the reader and when rechecking is necessary. A generic "all done" checkbox must not conceal unresolved blockers. Keep optional improvements separate from essential readiness.

Silently try the checklist against one normal case and one relevant failure while drafting. Can the buyer make the pass/fail decision from the stated criterion, and know what to do after a failure? Repair ambiguity in this turn without adding a user questionnaire or extra checkpoint. A checklist cannot certify legal, safety or professional compliance from guessed requirements.

## Visual Design

<!-- Modified 2026-11-02: design guidance adapted from Anthropic skills (Apache-2.0), pinned at ca45fc496a3436251f191354091d557da31c15b5. See THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md. -->

- Use a compact, readable hierarchy that matches the buyer’s actual checking situation. Group related checks and align each native checkbox with its observable criterion.
- Keep prerequisites, warnings and exception actions beside the checks they affect. Use spacing and selective emphasis to separate groups instead of giving every item a decorative card.
- Inspect the real Doc or PDF at intended reading size: readable labels, usable check areas, intact rows and balanced page breaks. This format does not inherit slide word or whitespace limits.

## Final Quality Review

- Try the checklist on a normal case and a failed check: each checkbox needs one observable condition and the reader must know whether to proceed or correct it.
- In the actual file, check usable checkboxes/response fields, readable grouping and page breaks that retain essential criteria beside their checks; correct safe layout defects.
- Keep required and optional checks distinct. Preserve approved criteria; report ambiguous or unsupported requirements and only grounded optional improvements under the shared review boundaries.
