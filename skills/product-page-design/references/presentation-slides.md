# Presentation slides

For a presentation, slide deck, slides or webinar, write one useful teaching beat per main section. Use a specific heading, concise complete teaching copy, and a real example or activity where it helps. A webinar includes the actual presentation and presenter notes by default; a script alone is insufficient. Keep the accumulated slide manuscript complete.

Use one `## Slide title` section per slide. Write the audience-facing content first, followed by `### Presenter notes` and the notes for that slide. Notes provide the explanation, demonstration instructions, audience prompts and transition as needed. Keep explanations that belong to the presenter in the explicitly labeled notes; do not arbitrarily move approved on-slide content into notes to reduce slide count.

The presentation destination is Canva, with presenter notes separate from the visible slide canvas. Verify the actual Canva pages and their native notes before returning the design as complete. The local fallback uses landscape 16:9 geometry for SVG preview and PDF; Markdown retains the complete source and notes. Visible content continues onto additional slides when needed, with the section title retained. To create shorter slides, revise the manuscript with the creator's approval. Do not offer Office downloads or assume a transfer to another editor preserves notes without checking.

Use ordered steps for sequence diagrams. Use an actual worked case for an example layout. Do not add fabricated numbers or charts. In Canva, retain editable text and useful native shapes rather than flattening the whole slide into an image. A creator-supplied photo is a raster image, with its aspect ratio preserved. Inspect the actual design and fonts in the destination editor before claiming layout or editability is verified.
