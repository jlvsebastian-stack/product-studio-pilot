# Design the reader's task

<!-- Modified 2026-11-02: design guidance adapted from Anthropic skills (Apache-2.0), pinned at ca45fc496a3436251f191354091d557da31c15b5. See THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md. -->

Keep brief.productDraft as the complete reader-facing manuscript. Design metadata never replaces, abbreviates or silently removes approved teaching, examples, exercise instructions or source URLs. Preserve the creator's wording unless they request revision. Use the current creator as the author.

Choose a visual purpose for each main section. Teaching explains a judgment, examples show its application, steps express a real sequence, exercises support a concrete action, checklists test observable criteria, and summaries identify the next useful action. Use diagrams only for relationships or ordered steps actually present in the text. Do not invent chart values, results or evidence for decoration.

Before expanding the first sample, state a concrete proposed visual direction based on this creator's preferences and invite a style/color change or reference. With no supplied direction, start with modern educational compositions: strong hierarchy, deliberate contrast and meaningful explanatory graphics. The app's interface palette and author credit are not the creator's brand. A generic title above pasted paragraphs is not a designed slide.

Establish art direction as well as information structure: a distinctive type pairing, coherent palette, image/illustration treatment, composition and rhythm. A correct branching diagram alone does not establish visual appeal. Show a representative rendered sample the creator can judge. If they reject the look, develop a substantially different visual direction instead of repeatedly restyling the same boxes. Use genuine, topic-appropriate imagery or a crafted visual concept where it adds meaning; keep audience text editable and spoken explanation in notes. A raster concept study is a visual reference, not a completed editable presentation or proof that the local app can reproduce it.

For presentations, first offer three lightweight template previews with distinct typography, composition and imagery treatment. The creator chooses ONE template before custom generation. One Create in Canva action reuses current original artwork or prepares only missing final-use illustrations, then creates exactly THREE actual editable samples in that style with full native notes. Review and approve those samples through their verified Canva link before expanding; do not insert local mockup approval or silently replace the chosen illustrated direction with an icon-only deck. Static template examples are not the creator's finished artwork. Use the chosen presentation skill and shared slide-art-direction contract. Every sample needs its own purpose, visual plan, artwork prompt, actual unique artwork and composition. Derive its visual takeaway from the learner's question, headline clues and source anchors, then describe concrete objects, actions and relationships in the prompt. Use at most14 total visible words with Title Case titles, and keep at least40% empty space. Retain the full canonical manuscript wording and original notes. More than one visual page may teach a source section, without inventing claims or rewriting the manuscript. Store choice and sample approval separately; neither implies a global preference.

Canva production must preserve the chosen concept, editable audience text and native per-slide presenter notes. Return a real editor link only after checking the actual design and reading its notes. If the native connection cannot write or verify notes, explain that precise limitation and retain the source; do not label an incomplete deck ready.

Compose the actual teaching mechanism: compare options in aligned columns/lanes, show explicit visitor journeys as paths, use native shapes and connections for real steps, and give an exercise a clear action/output area. Convert Markdown tables into a readable comparison, never raw pipe text. Vary composition by the reader's task rather than adding the same icon/panel to every slide. Preserve source wording and relationships; do not invent graphics' labels, data, screenshots or results. Genuine available images can illustrate a real need; do not claim generated images unless a verified image tool actually produced them.

Inspect the rendered sample at normal viewing size: labels and comparison cells must be readable, relationships must be clear, text must fit, and a concluding paragraph must not be stranded on an otherwise empty continuation. Reflow or rebalance within the source slide before splitting it; preserve approved content and its native notes. Judge the actual result, not a layout tag or a passing file-export test.

Put optional metadata in brief.designPlan as a JSON string:

```json
{"sections":[{"heading":"Exact manuscript section heading","layout":"steps","purpose":"Follow the actual process"}]}
```

Allowed manuscript layouts are teaching, steps, example, exercise, checklist and summary. Match existing headings and use a short purpose. Invalid or unknown metadata falls back to safe layout inference. Add no backstage layout instructions to productDraft. Local guide covers are automatic; presentation plans follow the actual source slides and do not add an automatic cover.

Read the format reference that matches the creator's deliverable: [guides and PDF](references/guides-pdf.md), [presentation slides](references/presentation-slides.md), or [Google Docs and editable text](references/word-text.md).

Use native editable lines, checkboxes, circles and text for suitable process and review graphics. An optional creator-supplied PNG or JPEG is separate from the manuscript and preference profile. Canva is the presentation destination and Google Docs the written-document destination. Do not claim image generation, a Canva design or its notes, a Google Workspace file, video or other native output was produced without actual evidence. Local PDF and Markdown are fallbacks, not completed native cloud files. Do not offer Microsoft Office downloads.

Before expanding a representative sample, use actual verified Canva or Google Docs links for review when connected, or the local PDF/Markdown fallback. Draft sample files are visibly marked DRAFT SAMPLE — for review and have -sample filenames. They require substantive current sample content but do not imply final approval, sale readiness or an actual native cloud upload. Bring the creator's feedback back into the current project before continuing.

## Design Direction

Use the creator's topic, intended buyer, practical task and approved references to choose a recognizable visual identity. Explicit creator preferences and an approved sample take precedence over defaults. Reuse what is already known; propose a direction within the existing sample conversation instead of adding a questionnaire. A project-specific design choice is not permission to remember an inferred preference.

Before making the existing sample or native file, settle a compact working design: palette roles with actual colors, one or two available font families and their roles, heading/body scale, spacing and alignment, and the visual treatment that fits this subject. Keep this inside existing design metadata or the production plan; no separate manifesto, theme catalog or extra approval. For slides, retain the selected one of the three concepts and its approved artwork.

Give the product one memorable visual idea grounded in its content, then keep supporting elements disciplined. Choose graphics that clarify a real comparison, process, relationship or example. Do not invent numbers, diagrams, screenshots or customer results for decoration. Preserve approved wording, source facts, formulas, artwork and notes. A design instruction does not authorize rewriting them.

Make hierarchy do useful work: unmistakable headings, readable body text, consistent alignment and spacing, meaningful emphasis, and labels beside what they describe. Use numbering for actual sequences. Avoid turning every paragraph into the same card, adding ornamental labels everywhere, or applying the app's cream/terracotta styling to every creator. A familiar palette or layout is acceptable when this creator chose it; arbitrary novelty is not the goal.

Build for the chosen native destination with editable text, real headings/tables/cells and supported fonts. Canvas-design informs composition for static PNG/PDF art; it is not a Canva connector or a substitute for editable Canva slides and native notes. Do not make raster mockups as an additional preview step.

Critique the existing sample and final output against the brief: check focal point, image-to-message fit, contrast, alignment, legibility, bounds, density and page breaks at actual reading size. Remove needless decoration and fix authorized formatting in the same production pass. Readback establishes native structure; only inspecting rendered pages establishes visual evidence. State what remains unverified. These instructions improve decisions, not guarantee beauty. Each format keeps its own density rules; the 14-word and 40% empty-space limits apply only to presentation/webinar slides.
